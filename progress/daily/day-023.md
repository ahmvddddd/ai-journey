## Lessons Learned

* **Cancellation & Units:** Squaring deviations prevents positive and negative differences from canceling out to zero ($\sum (x_i - \bar{x}) = 0$), but introduces squared units. Taking the square root restores original unit metrics.
* **Bessel's Correction ($n - 1$):** Estimating the population mean using a sample mean artificially minimizes the sum of squared errors. Dividing by $n - 1$ corrects this downward bias, producing an unbiased variance estimator ($E[s^2] = \sigma^2$).
* **Library Default Nuance:** NumPy defaults to population statistics (`ddof=0`), whereas Pandas defaults to sample statistics (`ddof=1`). Setting explicit `ddof` parameters is crucial for cross-library consistency.
